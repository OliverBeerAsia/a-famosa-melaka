# Contact shadows — benchmark item 13

`assets/sprites/effects/contact-shadows.png`
Generated by `tools/forge/contact-shadows.cjs`. **Do not hand-edit the PNG** —
re-run the tool. It is deterministic; same source, same bytes.

---

## What this fixes

A composited sprite with nothing under it reads as a **decal pasted onto a
photograph**, not as a body standing on ground. This is the most common failure
of 2.5D pixel art and the reason benchmark item 13 exists.

The *painted* props on every plate already solve it: `compose-plate.cjs` calls
`primitives.cjs contactShadow()` and bakes an ellipse under each one. A moving
sprite cannot use a baked shadow — the shadow has to move with it — so moving
actors need a shadow **sprite**, and this sheet is it.

| covered by | mechanism |
|---|---|
| props, buildings, kit pieces | baked at compose time (`primitives.cjs contactShadow`) |
| player, NPCs, crowd, items, fauna | **this sheet**, drawn per frame by the engine |

---

## The sheet

```
120 x 20  ·  three 40 x 20 frames  ·  left to right: small, medium, large
```

| frame | for an actor | ellipse | % of foot | implied opacity | offset from anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 `small` | 16 px wide | 12 x 6 | 75 % | 40 % | +3, +2 |
| 1 `medium` | 24 px wide | 18 x 9 | 75 % | 42 % | +4, +2 |
| 2 `large` | 32 px wide | 24 x 12 | 75 % | 39 % | +5, +3 |

All native px. Frames share one size because Phaser slices a sheet on a fixed
grid — three differently-sized frames cannot be loaded as one spritesheet.

### Why each number is what it is

- **2:1.** The shadow lies on the ground and the ground is a 2:1 isometric
  plane. A circle reads as a ball under the feet.
- **75 % of foot width.** The bar is ≥ 60 %; 60 % of a 16 px actor is 9.6 px,
  and a shadow with an odd half-pixel has no centre. 75 % rounds clean at all
  three sizes and stays comfortably over the bar.
- **Opacity is DITHER DENSITY, not alpha.** Alpha is 0 or 255 everywhere. A
  partial-alpha shadow is anti-aliasing, and `validate-canon.cjs` fails builds
  over exactly that. Density carries the value instead: ~62 % of pixels set in
  the core, 34 % in the mid band, 16 % in the skirt — averaging inside the
  25–45 % window while giving the falloff real pixel texture.
- **Two tones, never black.** Core is `shadow-void` `#0C0C18`, skirt is
  `shadow-violet` `#181830`. Pure black is not in the canon: on a warm plate it
  reads as a hole punched in the ground.
- **The NW sun is baked in.** `palette.cjs SUN` is azimuth 315 / elevation 30,
  so the shadow falls **down-right**. That displacement lives inside the frame
  (see the table), which means a consumer just centres the frame on the feet and
  *cannot* get the sun direction wrong by passing the wrong sign.

## Intended engine usage

**Not yet wired.** This is a data-only delivery; the wiring is an engine task.
When it lands, this is the contract the art was authored against:

```
load        this.load.spritesheet('contact-shadow',
              'sprites/effects/contact-shadows.png',
              { frameWidth: 40, frameHeight: 20 })

frame       0 for actors up to 16 native px wide (crowd, items, small fauna)
            1 for 24 px (the player and named NPCs at CHARACTER_SCALE)
            2 for 32 px (carts, large fauna, anything with a wide stance)

place       origin (0.5, 0.5) at the actor's FOOT ANCHOR — the same (x, y) the
            sprite's own (0.5, 1) origin sits on. Add no offset of your own;
            the sun offset is already in the frame.

scale       the same scale as the actor (CHARACTER_SCALE, 3). Never scale the
            shadow independently — the dither would stop lining up with the
            world's pixel grid, which is the whole point of a native asset.

depth       worldDepth(y) - 1. Under its own actor, over the plate, and it must
            sort with the actor so it can never be drawn on top of a sprite
            standing in front of it.

tint        none at day. At other hours take the tint from
            src/data/relight-runtime.json exactly as the character sprite does —
            a shadow that stays day-coloured at night is the double-grade
            coming back through the back door.

never       do not fade the shadow with alpha to suggest a softer light. Alpha
            is 0 or 255. If a softer contact is wanted, that is a new frame with
            a lower dither density, generated by the tool.
```

Flying fauna (butterfly, swift, kite) get **no** contact shadow — they never
land, and per `docs/design/game-feel-spec.md` §4.3 the shadow is required only
for ground-dwelling actors.

## Directory status

`assets/sprites/effects/` is **not** yet in `gameplay-asset-spec.json`'s
`allowedDirectories`, so `validate-gameplay-assets.cjs` emits one `warn` for
this file. That is accurate, not an oversight: the directory becomes a promoted
shipping class when Stage 5 moves the particles into it and the engine actually
draws from it. `validate-canon.cjs` already lists the path on
`LEGACY_ALLOWLIST` for the same stage.

## Regenerating

```bash
node tools/forge/contact-shadows.cjs           # render + install
node tools/forge/contact-shadows.cjs --check   # gates only, writes nothing
node tools/forge/contact-shadows.cjs --sheet   # + docs/art-bible/forge/review/contact-shadows.png
```

The tool's own gates fail non-zero on: any partial alpha, any colour that is not
one of the two anchors, more or fewer than two tones, an ellipse under 60 % of
its foot width, a ratio that is not 2:1, an implied opacity outside 25–45 %, or
an ellipse that is not displaced down-right of the anchor.
